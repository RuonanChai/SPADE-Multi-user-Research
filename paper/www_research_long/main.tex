\title{Browser Range Admission, Not Cache Replacement, Sets the 3DGS First-Byte Wait}
\begin{abstract}
Matched headed Ethernet experiments show that a shared LRU does not remove the multi-hundred-millisecond browser send-to-first-byte wait. Restricting each browser to one outstanding Range request lowers that median and also lowers completed 206 responses. The same cap is not distinct from setting the fetch slot count to one. Overlapping response bodies does not restore the lost completions, because the body after the first byte is short. Visible-splat progress is measured from the light timeline. PSNR and SSIM are not.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle
\section{Introduction}
The measured delay sits in each browser's own Range admission. Changing the first hop to a direct proxy or to HTTP/2 did not remove it. Cache hit savings therefore do not identify the wait.
\section{Method}
The scene is coit-40m-sh1-lod and the camera trace is stop-go. The cache is a shared 50\,MiB, 100-entry LRU. Clients are headed Chrome on Ethernet. SPADE v1 admits one currently demanded Range. The matched control is the same renderer with the fetch slot count set to one, without a separate scheduler name. Light instrumentation records request lifecycle and does not capture screenshots. PSNR and SSIM are defined only for cells later marked in a frozen sampling manifest.
\section{Results}
Vite LRU with three slots produces N=2 send-to-first medians of 442/403\,ms and 474/439\,ms, with about 370--390 completed 206 responses per client. SPADE v1 produces 181/168\,ms and 166/158\,ms on N=2, and 173/168/147\,ms and 173/176/146\,ms on N=3, while completed 206 responses fall to about 270--320. Matched LRU slots=1 agrees: N=3 medians are 169/157/122\,ms and 175/157/122\,ms, with 206 counts 282/289/334 and 277/287/325, and header-wait depth stays 1. LRU slots=2 is the strongest tuned baseline. Its N=2 medians are near 246/227\,ms and 273/230\,ms, with about 367--384 completed 206 responses.
A header-release rule with one headers-pending request and one active body reached those peaks, but its N=2 medians stayed at 167/157\,ms and 181/172\,ms and its 206 counts stayed at 294/289 and 262/274. On the slots=2 N=2 first repetition, client C3 has send-to-first p50/p95 of 246/374\,ms and send-to-complete p50/p95 of 270/403\,ms, with 384 windowed 206 responses. The body-transfer p95 after the first byte is 70\,ms. Mean visible splats reach about 3.0 million in the first 5\,s of the saved timeline and about 3.75 million by 10\,s. Time to the first visible splat and time to one million splats are left-censored: the first saved sample is already past both thresholds. Per-client GPU utilization averages about 97 percent. Display presentation, PSNR, and SSIM remain unmeasured. These measurements are not a confirmatory claim.
The time after the first byte does not set the completion gap. On N=2 client C3, body transfer medians stay between 18 and 34\,ms, worker decode medians stay between 58 and 63\,ms, and GPU upload medians stay below 1\,ms. Admission-to-decode medians are 221/217\,ms with one slot, 304/340\,ms with two slots, and 503/531\,ms with three slots. Header release keeps that median at 221/231\,ms and keeps the one-slot fetch count. The trace moves for about 3\,s and then rests for about 7\,s. Raising the cap from one slot to two adds fetches in both intervals. On N=2 C3 the two repetitions add 23 and 21 fetches during motion and 22 and 19 during rest. On N=3 C5 they add 12 and 13 during motion and 28 and 25 during rest. A gate that withholds the second slot while the camera is still would remove the larger part of the measured gain.
On the same N=2 C3 chunks, the median wait from reaching the front of the unfetched list to admission is 276\,ms with one slot, 189\,ms and 209\,ms with two slots, and 192\,ms with three slots. The third slot does not remove that wait. Decode on those chunks stays near 62\,ms. Only 7 to 9 percent of two-slot decodes overlap another decode. The GPU upload registration stays under 1\,ms, but on the same chunk it starts a median 2.4\,s after decode in the earlier logs. An instrumented slots=2 canary splits that interval without pooling it into the baseline. Decode to the CPU upload queue is under 1\,ms. The same chunk and page then wait 818\,ms on C3 and 705\,ms on C4 for the LOD worker to accept them. The tree update is 7\,ms and 6\,ms. The following 1703\,ms on C3 and 1518\,ms on C4 are chunk-weighted medians of one shared callback wall, measured on 79 and 77 batches. The batch-equal medians are 1560\,ms and 1409\,ms, and uploads inside one batch spread by less than 10\,ms. Spacing between LOD updates matches that wall, so the callbacks run back-to-back. The canary has no traversal-end timestamp, so the wall still mixes the awaited display traversal with the draw-index upload. Those medians are not added to the queue wait. Among batch gaps covered by a 1\,Hz camera sample, 22 of 43 on C3 and 22 of 44 on C4 have an unchanged camera, and 30 of 43 and 31 of 44 fall in a rest interval. The renderer still marks the LOD tree dirty on every chunk update. The predicted camera delta is computed and then not applied, so a still camera does not skip the walk. A later traverse-split attempt did not reach the barrier. C4 had already written a local READY file. The controller only searched the SSH probe text for the word READY and never copied or parsed that file, so it did not release the barrier. Chrome then reported a disconnected network, and every traversal in that log has zero LOD splats. Its sub-millisecond times are only a negative control for fixed callback overhead. An unchanged camera position is also not sufficient to skip a rebuild: orientation, field of view, viewport, LOD threshold, and tree version are separate inputs. A later loaded-scene cell reaches 3{,}750{,}000 LOD splats. The awaited display traversal, timed on one clock around the worker call, has median 830\,ms on C3 ($n{=}105$) and 1293\,ms on C4 ($n{=}96$). After that call returns, building the draw index takes 1.4\,ms and 2.4\,ms. The raycast traversal medians are 6\,ms and 10\,ms. This awaited interval is a worker round trip, not a CPU profile and not the main-thread index upload, so maintaining that index incrementally does not remove the wall. A following cell times the worker itself. Around the synchronous {\tt traverse\_lod\_trees} call the median is 814\,ms on C3 ($n{=}119$) and 1243\,ms on C4 ($n{=}105$), while argument preparation, result packing, and the remainder for both messages are 0\,ms, 0\,ms, and 0.5\,ms. The index buffer is transferred rather than copied. Of the walks that take at least 500\,ms, 52 of 64 on C3 and 44 of 63 on C4 do not repeat the previous rounded view; among those that do, a tree update is almost always present (12 of 12 and 18 of 19). Skipping an unchanged view therefore does not remove the wall. The worker source expands a binary heap of pixel scales and then copies a 16384-padded index; a 4-heap in the same file is unused, and the index sort is commented out. A release-optimized reimplementation of that loop, checked against a leaf-set oracle, spends 59.7 of 60.3\,ms in the expansion at 349{,}525 node visits and 267 of 270\,ms at 1{,}398{,}101 visits. The same 349{,}525-node tree takes 0.002\,ms when the pixel limit stops at the root. Allocating and copying a 15\,MB index in the host runtime takes 4.8\,ms. The visit count, not the mere presence of the tree, sets this cost. On one fixed 4-ary tree of 5{,}592{,}405 nodes, raising only the splat cap from 200{,}000 to 3{,}750{,}000 raises the host time from 15.8\,ms to 363\,ms and the pixel tests from 266{,}665 to 4{,}999{,}997. A 40-unit camera move keeps those 4{,}999{,}997 tests and changes the selected indices. Expansion is 349\,ms of the 363\,ms; draining the frontier and copying the padded index are 5.5\,ms and 2.1\,ms. A pixel limit of 0.001 stops this synthetic tree at 262{,}144 splats, but the browser does not stop there. Every paired display walk in the worker-timed cell uses a cap of 3{,}750{,}000 and a pixel-scale limit of 0.002131. Of the 119 and 105 paired walks, the 21 and 24 with a selected count between one and 3.3 million have worker medians of 1291\,ms and 1267\,ms. The 41 and 39 walks at or above 3.3 million have medians 1666\,ms and 1551\,ms, and those selections have medians 3{,}749{,}996 and 3{,}749{,}997. Empty selections take 0.1\,ms. The host times are not the browser wasm times, and this tree treats every child as resident. Compiling that same resident walk with wasm-pack release and wasm-opt, then calling it from Node after one warmup, takes 358\,ms, 364\,ms, and 363\,ms and returns 3{,}749{,}998 indices. The wasm runtime of this 4-ary walk matches the host and does not account for the browser medians above. A camera move that preserves the selected set is not available once the budget is the stop, so no such mechanism is frozen. CPU registration is not a GPU fence. Three.js can send a marked data-array layer on a later render, and the canary does not time that call. Once per second, the submitted index buffer names the chunk with no unmapped page. That record is draw inclusion, not display presentation.
A release host model of the production walk, using the streaming cone of 70/120 degrees and foveation weights 0.2 and 0.4, rejects three exact-set shortcuts. On one uniform 4-ary tree of 5{,}592{,}405 resident nodes and a cap of 3{,}750{,}000, the warm expansion medians are 404\,ms for the live binary heap, 296\,ms with precomputed pixel scales, 375\,ms for the unused 4-heap, 382\,ms without the touched-chunk set, and 303\,ms when each node is packed with its scale. Three repetitions produce those medians. The sorted index set matches in every arm. The 4-heap raw checksum does not match, because the budget stop leaves all 3{,}749{,}998 indices in heap-drain order and that drain is not sorted. The same tree at the browser pixel-scale limit 0.002131 emits 65{,}536 splats in 4.2\,ms, so this synthetic geometry is not the browser walk. The expansion still performs 4{,}999{,}997 pixel evaluations and 1{,}249{,}999 pops; those counts follow from replacing one node by four children until the next replacement would exceed the cap. Skipping the scale arithmetic, the chunk-touch set, or the splat layout therefore leaves about 300\,ms, and none of the preregistered cuts (below 60\%, 80\%, and 50\% of the live expansion) is met. These are host times, not Chrome times. No scheduler is frozen from this comparison.
\section{Limitations}
One scene and one trajectory cannot support generalization. Saved timelines give visible-splat progress, but the first sample is already late, so time-to-first-visible and time-to-one-million-splats stay left-censored. The instrumented canary records draw inclusion from the submitted index buffer. Its page map has no allocation generation, so a recycled page is not yet separated from the index that named it. CPU texture registration is not GPU completion, and draw inclusion is not display presentation. PSNR and SSIM are not measured. No frozen confirmation matrix is authorized.
